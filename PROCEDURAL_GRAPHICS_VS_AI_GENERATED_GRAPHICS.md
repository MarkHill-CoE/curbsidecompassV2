## 2. Defining the Two Technologies

### 2.1 What Are Procedural Graphics?
**Procedural graphics** are visual assets, scenes, and animations generated algorithmically through deterministic mathematical code rather than loaded from pre-rendered raster files (like PNG, JPEG, or WebP) or generated by neural networks.

In the Curbside Compass codebase (`/src/components/NeighborhoodSimulation.tsx`):
- Every building, tree, fire hydrant, transit bus, delivery van, and pedestrian is constructed from **mathematical vector formulas** executing directly in the user’s web browser.
- Geometry is calculated on an HTML5 `<canvas>` using 3D world coordinates $(x, y, z)$ projected into 2.5D isometric screen coordinates via trigonometric functions ($\cos 30^\circ$, $\sin 30^\circ$).
- Lighting, shadows, perspective depth, and animations are strictly derived from algebraic functions and physical rules (e.g., sunlight vectors, car velocity equations, collision hitboxes, and obstacle queues).

```typescript
// Example from NeighborhoodSimulation.tsx: Deterministic procedural cuboid
function drawBlock(x, y, z, w, d, h, topColor, leftColor, rightColor) {
  // Pure mathematical projection: Every coordinate is calculated deterministically
  const p2_x = (x + w - y) * ISO_X + offsetX;
  const p2_y = (x + w + y) * ISO_Y - (z * ISO_Z) + offsetY;
  // Canvas fills exact vector paths (Right face, Left face, Top face)
  targetCtx.fillStyle = rightColor;
  targetCtx.beginPath();
  targetCtx.moveTo(p3_x, p3_y);
  // ...
  targetCtx.fill();
}
```

#### Key Characteristics of Procedural Graphics:
- **100% Deterministic:** Given the exact same inputs (e.g., 1.8 cars per home, 1 garage space, mature laned typology), the output will always render the exact same geometry down to the sub-pixel.
- **Parametrically Bound:** Objects can be dynamically resized, recolored, repositioned, or animated on the fly at 60 frames per second.
- **Zero Asset Download:** No image files are fetched over the network; the entire neighborhood engine is contained in lightweight JavaScript/TypeScript code.
- **Direct State Binding:** When a survey respondent moves a slider or selects a policy option, the procedural model updates instantly without re-generating or reloading assets.

### 2.2 What Are AI / LLM / Diffusion-Generated Graphics?
**AI-generated graphics** (such as those produced by diffusion models like Imagen, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney, or DALL-E, often orchestrated via LLM prompts) are raster bitmap images synthesized through probabilistic pattern recognition.

#### How Generative AI Image Synthesis Works:
1. **Neural Pattern Matching:** Deep neural networks are trained on millions of labeled images to learn statistical associations between words (e.g., *"Edmonton residential street with suburban homes and parked cars"*) and pixel color arrangements.
2. **Latent Space Sampling:** The model begins with random Gaussian visual noise and iteratively "denoises" the latent space across dozens of diffusion steps until a plausible image emerges.
3. **Probabilistic Hallucination:** The model does not know what a street is, what Bylaw 5590 requires, or how many cars fit on a block. It simply predicts pixel color probabilities that match the statistical distribution of its training data.

#### Key Characteristics of AI-Generated Graphics:
- **Stochastic (Non-Deterministic):** Running the exact same prompt twice yields two completely different images with different perspectives, building shapes, and vehicle counts.
- **Static Raster Output:** Produces a flat grid of pixels (JPEG/PNG/WebP). Individual cars, trees, or curbs cannot be selected, animated, or tied to variable states.
- **High Computational & Latency Cost:** Generating a single image requires intensive server-side GPU processing (typically taking 2 to 10 seconds per image).
- **Lack of Physical Grounding:** Models frequently introduce spatial inconsistencies, such as vehicles merging into sidewalks, nonsensical wheel counts, floating street signs, or impossible curb geometry.

## 3. Side-by-Side Comparison: Procedural vs. AI-Generated

| Evaluation Dimension | Procedural Vector Graphics (Curbside Compass Engine) | Generative AI / Diffusion Graphics (LLM-Prompted) |
|---|---|---|
| **Underlying Mechanism** | Algorithmic code, trigonometric projection, vector geometry | Deep neural networks, statistical noise diffusion, latent space sampling |
| **Output Format** | Live vector rendering on HTML5 Canvas / SVG (resolution-independent) | Fixed-resolution raster bitmap (e.g., 1024×1024 PNG / JPEG) |
| **Physical & Metric Accuracy** | **Exact:** A 6.0m stall is exactly 16.0 units; a 15.6m lot is exactly 30.5 units; 1.5m driveway setbacks match Edmonton Bylaw 5590 | **Approximate / Hallucinatory:** Cannot guarantee physical dimensions, stall lengths, or regulatory distances |
| **Interactivity & Frame Rate** | **Live 60 FPS:** Smooth vehicle movement, flashing turn signals, pedestrian walking, and police traffic directing | **Static / 0 FPS:** Static images; cannot support real-time user-controlled interactive simulation |
| **State Responsiveness** | **Instantaneous (< 1 millisecond):** Changing a slider immediately moves cars into garages or onto the curb | **Slow (2,000 – 10,000 ms per image):** Requires calling external cloud GPU APIs for every tiny change |
| **Determinism & Reproducibility** | **100% Deterministic:** Identical inputs yield mathematically identical visual states every time | **Stochastic:** Generates different streets, house counts, and cars on every run |
| **Auditability & Explainability** | **Fully Auditable:** Every polygon, line of code, and coordinate is inspectable by municipal auditors | **Black Box:** Pixel synthesis inside billions of neural weights cannot be audited or explained to city council |
| **Bandwidth & File Size** | **0 kB download:** Code is bundled directly into the web application; zero external image assets | **Heavy:** Requires transferring 500 kB to 3 MB raster files per scene over the user's cellular/Wi-Fi connection |
| **Privacy & Data Sovereignty** | **100% Client-Side:** Runs locally in browser memory; zero user data or prompts leave the user's device | **Server-Side APIs:** Requires sending prompt contexts to cloud servers, introducing potential privacy/data-sovereignty concerns |
| **City Standards Compliance** | Programmed strictly to **Edmonton Bylaw 5590**, **Complete Streets Volume 2**, and **ETS Bus Stop Standards** | Unaware of local Edmonton bylaws; trained on generic global internet photography |
| **Device Compatibility** | Runs smoothly on low-end municipal laptops, Chromebooks, and budget smartphones | Heavy browser memory usage if rendering video or waiting on high-latency cloud connections |

## 4. Why Procedural Graphics Are Necessary for Municipal Simulations

### 4.1 Enforcing Real Municipal Bylaws & Standards
In urban parking policy, **exact spatial numbers matter**. For example:
- **City of Edmonton Traffic Bylaw 5590, Section 37:** Prohibits parking within **1.5 metres** of any driveway. In the procedural engine, the code mathematically calculates `startX + 13.5 - 1.5` and paints a 1.5-unit yellow no-parking zone with diagonal clearance lines on either side of each driveway.
- **Fire Hydrant Clearance:** Prohibits parking within **5.0 metres** of a hydrant. The procedural code places a yellow curb zone spanning exactly 10 coordinate units with white limit ticks and a 5m bylaw plate.
- **ETS Transit Bus Stop Zone:** Requires a **30.0-metre** clear curb zone for 40ft low-floor buses. The procedural code reserves an 80-coordinate-unit span ($x = 295$ to $375$) with yellow warning stencils.

> **Why AI Fails Here:** A generative AI model cannot reliably enforce a 1.5-metre clearance or an exact 30-metre transit zone. It routinely generates cars parked directly across fire hydrants, cars overlapping driveways, or arbitrary curb lengths that misrepresent Edmonton bylaws to the public.

---

### 4.2 Dynamic Simulation & State Synchronization
The entire purpose of the Curbside Compass is to let citizens and planners explore **cause and effect**:
- *Question:* "What happens if every home has 2.4 cars instead of 1.2?"
- *Procedural Response:* The engine calculates that curbside demand increases by 12 vehicles. It checks off-street garage capacities, parks overflow cars in legal street stalls, and when the street reaches 100% capacity, initiates **real-time circling vehicles** that physically drive around the block searching for a spot.
- *Question:* "What if an 8-plex missing-middle infill replaces a single-family home?"
- *Procedural Response:* The procedural engine replaces lot #2 with a 3-storey building footprint, removes the driveway curb cuts, restores the legal curbside stalls, adds a 4-bike front yard rack, and dynamically adjusts resident vehicle counts.

> **Why AI Fails Here:** Generative AI cannot execute a state machine. It cannot simulate physics, cannot model queueing theory, cannot manage vehicle velocity, and cannot animate cars yielding at intersections.

---

### 4.3 Resolution Independence & Performance
The procedural engine uses geometric vector math:
- Whether viewed on a 4K desktop monitor, an iPad Retina display, or a compact smartphone screen, the canvas scales cleanly via viewport scaling without pixelation, compression artifacts, or blurry textures.
- The entire visual simulation runs within a lightweight **3,000-polygon budget**, utilizing offscreen canvas caching (`bgGroundCanvas`, `bgHousesCanvas`, `bgTreesCanvas`) so that only moving vehicles and pedestrians re-render on active frames, consuming **less than 3% CPU utilization**.

---

### 4.4 Ethical Governance & Explainability (City of Edmonton AI Standard)
The City of Edmonton's **Generative AI Standard** and municipal governance frameworks require transparency, accountability, and explainability in public engagement tools:
- **No Hallucinations:** In a procedural model, every graphic element corresponds directly to empirical data or established policy assumptions. There is no risk of the engine "hallucinating" inappropriate imagery, offensive signs, or inaccurate city branding.
- **Full Source Code Auditability:** City auditors, council members, and community leagues can inspect the exact lines of code that determine where cars park and how congestion is visualized.
- **POPA / FOIP Privacy Compliance:** Because the procedural engine executes locally in the resident's browser without sending user inputs to third-party generative AI cloud endpoints, resident privacy is completely protected under Alberta's *Protection of Privacy Act (POPA)* and *Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act (FOIP)*.

|---|---|
| **Municipal Precision** | Ensures Edmonton Bylaw 5590 curb setbacks (1.5m) and ETS bus stop zones (30m) are mathematically accurate. |
| **Real-Time Responsiveness** | Updates street graphics instantly (< 1ms) as residents answer policy survey questions. |

In short, **generative AI creates artistic representations of what a street *might look like*, while procedural graphics create an authentic mathematical model of how a street *actually behaves*.** For civic consultation, public policy evaluation, and municipal credibility, procedural graphics represent the gold standard.
